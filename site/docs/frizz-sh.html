Remote access

Frizz subdomain

An address on frizz.sh that reaches your board from another device. It needs no account, no domain, and nothing installed.

Setup

  1. Press R in the terminal running Frizz.
  2. Choose frizz.sh subdomain.
  3. Answer what a device signed in through frizz.sh can do: Full control or Read and answer.
  4. Confirm that the board goes on the internet.
  5. Scan the QR code with your phone.
Serving https://mk7w2qp9x4n3f6tbz5vh.frizz.sh (frizz.sh).

Scan to sign in on a phone. Single use, expires in 5 minutes. Press L later for another.

What a signed-in device can do

A device with full control can do what a tab on the computer running Frizz can do. A few things stay on that computer for every device:

  • minting a sign-in link;
  • listing the devices that hold a session, or signing another one out;
  • changing what a device signed in through frizz.sh can do.

A device at read and answer reads the board and answers question cards by picking a listed option. Everything else happens on the computer running Frizz:

  • starting or steering a thread, or changing its settings;
  • answering a free-text, secret or danger card;
  • approving or denying an action a worker is waiting on;
  • restarts and updates;
  • reading a file outside a registered project or a thread’s scratch and attachment directories, or a secret-looking file inside one (.env, a key, anything under a dotted folder other than .frizz).

To change the answer later, press R and pick What they can do under Devices. The new answer applies to devices already signed in. A refused call is written to logs/audit.jsonl under Frizz’s data folder, with the session and device that made it.

Connection

This option needs no Tailscale or Cloudflare Tunnel. Your board stays on localhost and opens one outbound connection to a relay Frizz runs, and visitors’ requests travel down that connection.

  • No inbound port. The board dials out, so a laptop behind any NAT works with no configuration.
  • No DNS record. The relay already answers for every frizz.sh subdomain.
  • Reconnects. The connection comes back after a sleep or a network change.

Your subdomain

Address20 random characters, so nobody finds it by guessing. A first visit still needs a single-use sign-in link.
OwnershipA key on your machine proves ownership, with no account. Copy the key file to another machine to move the subdomain.
Key fileThe file is identity.key, in ~/Library/Application Support/Frizz on macOS and ~/.local/state/frizz on Linux. An older install keeps it in ~/.frizz.
RenewalA running board renews it. A subdomain nobody runs for 30 days is released, and someone else can claim it.

Terms

  • One per machine. Each machine key holds one subdomain.
  • Lease. Frizz can reclaim any subdomain at any time.
  • Relay. Every request and keystroke passes through a relay Frizz runs, and the sign-in check runs on your machine. A device with full control can run anything on your machine. The relay carries that traffic, so anyone who took control of the relay could do the same. Under read and answer, they could read the board and pick options on cards. To keep the relay out of the path, use Tailscale or your own proxy.
  • No warranty. If you can’t afford to lose access, use a domain you control.

Other devices

Each device signs in with its own single-use link, which gives it a session for 30 days. Press L in the terminal for a new link, or run this from another shell:

$ npx frizz --link

To see the devices holding a session, or to revoke them, run these on the computer running Frizz:

$ npx frizz --sessions
$ npx frizz --sign-out all

A device can sign itself out: Sign out this device in Settings ends that browser’s session and nothing else. Signing out any other device happens on the computer running Frizz, so a stolen phone cannot sign out the laptop.
